# Can a Guest House Generate €1,000 a Month?

A guest house can be more than accommodation for family and friends. In the right location, it can also become a useful source of additional income.

But can it realistically generate **€1,000 a month**?

The simple answer is: potentially, yes. The calculation is surprisingly straightforward.

At an average nightly rate of €100, you need:

**€100 × 10 nights = €1,000**

Over a full year, that means 120 booked nights to generate €12,000 in gross rental revenue.

That does not mean every guest house can achieve this. Location, demand, pricing, seasonality and operating costs all matter. But the target itself is not particularly high in terms of required occupancy.

## How Many Nights Do You Need?

The nightly rate makes a big difference:

* **€80 per night:** 13 nights per month
* **€100 per night:** 10 nights per month
* **€125 per night:** 8 nights per month

At €100 per night, 120 booked nights represents an average occupancy of around **33% across the year**.

In practice, bookings will rarely be spread evenly. A guest house might be busy during summer and holidays, while quieter months generate fewer bookings. Looking at the annual total is therefore more useful than expecting €1,000 every single month.

## What Determines the Income?

### Location

Location is one of the biggest factors.

A guest house near a lake, coastline, ski area, national park or popular hiking destination may attract visitors specifically looking for a quiet place to stay.

It doesn't necessarily need to be in a major city. For some guests, being surrounded by nature is the reason for the booking.

### Nightly Rate

The right price depends on the property and the experience it offers.

A basic guest house in a low-demand area may need more booked nights to reach €12,000. A well-designed property with features such as a private sauna, outdoor space or attractive natural surroundings may be able to charge more.

Guests are not only paying for square metres. They are also paying for the experience.

### Occupancy

At €100 per night, 120 nights generates €12,000 in annual gross revenue.

That could mean a few booked nights each week rather than continuous occupancy.

The important question is therefore not whether the guest house can be rented every night, but whether the location and property can consistently attract enough bookings throughout the year.

## Revenue Is Not Profit

€12,000 in annual rental revenue does not mean €12,000 in profit.

There may be costs for:

* Cleaning and laundry
* Electricity, heating and water
* Internet
* Maintenance and repairs
* Insurance
* Booking platform fees
* Taxes

The actual result depends on how the property is operated. Owners who handle cleaning, guest communication and maintenance themselves may have a different cost structure from those who outsource everything.

Tax treatment also varies between countries, so rental revenue and taxable profit should not be treated as the same thing.

## Our Own Experience

We have operated a guest house at approximately **€100 per night** and have generated more than **€12,000 per year** in rental revenue.

That experience does not mean the same result can be expected everywhere. Demand and pricing vary significantly between locations.

It does, however, show that a relatively small number of booked nights can create meaningful additional income when the property is well positioned and the operating costs are manageable.

A guest house also doesn't have to be used exclusively as a rental. It can remain available for family and friends, personal holidays or other uses when it isn't being rented.

## Is €1,000 a Month Realistic?

**It can be.**

At €100 per night, the target requires 120 booked nights per year. That is roughly one third of the year.

The challenge is not necessarily achieving high occupancy. It is finding the right combination of **location, pricing, property and demand**.

A guest house is not completely passive income, but it can become a relatively low-effort source of additional revenue when the operation is set up well.

And that is perhaps the most interesting part: **you don't need to rent it every night to make the numbers work.**

## FAQ

### How many nights are needed to make €1,000 a month?

At €100 per night, you need 10 booked nights per month on average. At €125 per night, you need 8.

### Is €12,000 in rental revenue the same as profit?

No. Cleaning, utilities, maintenance, platform fees, insurance and taxes can all reduce the amount left after expenses.

### Does a guest house need high occupancy to generate €1,000 a month?

Not necessarily. At €100 per night, 120 booked nights per year are enough to generate €12,000 in gross annual revenue, equivalent to around 33% occupancy across the year.
