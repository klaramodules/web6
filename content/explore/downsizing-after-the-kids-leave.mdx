# Downsizing After the Kids Leave: A Different Way to Think About Home

A family home can be exactly what you need for 20 or 30 years.

Then the children leave.

Suddenly, the four bedrooms, large garden and extra living spaces that once felt necessary can start to feel like a lot of house for two people.

This is where downsizing comes in.

But downsizing does not necessarily have to mean selling everything and moving somewhere else.

## Europe Has a Lot of Underused Space

The numbers are striking.

According to Eurostat, **33% of people in the EU lived in an under-occupied home in 2024** — meaning the home was considered larger than the household's needs.

Eurostat specifically identifies older individuals or couples remaining in their homes after their children have grown up and left as the classic cause of under-occupation. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The situation varies considerably between countries. Under-occupation was particularly common in countries such as Cyprus, Ireland and Malta. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

That does not mean everyone in a large home wants to move.

Quite the opposite.

A 2024 study from the Zurich University of Applied Sciences found that empty nesters have the greatest potential for downsizing, but that this potential is only being used to a limited extent. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

## Why Don't People Downsize?

Because the house is often more than just a building.

It may be the place where the family grew up.

It may have a large garden, a workshop, a view, a favourite neighbourhood or decades of memories attached to it.

Moving to a smaller apartment can therefore feel like giving up much more than square metres.

There is also a practical problem: suitable smaller homes are not always easy to find.

So some people simply stay.

## What If You Kept the Place — But Lived Smaller?

There is another possibility.

Instead of selling the family home, imagine building a small, independent home on the same property.

The original house could eventually be sold, rented out, used by children or grandchildren, or simply remain part of the family's property.

The smaller home becomes the place where the parents actually live.

This could provide many of the advantages of downsizing without requiring a move to a completely different location.

## Less House, More Freedom

A smaller home generally means less space to heat, clean and maintain.

It can also mean fewer things to manage.

For a couple who spends more time travelling, this can be particularly attractive.

Instead of spending retirement maintaining a large house, they could have a compact home that is relatively simple to look after.

The goal is not necessarily to own less for the sake of owning less.

It is to make the home fit the life you actually have now.

## Downsizing Can Also Free Up Capital

There is another reason people consider downsizing.

A large family home can represent a significant amount of wealth.

Moving from a larger property to a smaller one can potentially release some of that capital.

The exact result depends on property prices, transaction costs, taxes, financing and the cost of the new home.

But the basic idea is simple:

**Less house can mean more capital available for other things.**

That could mean travel, investments, helping children or simply having greater financial flexibility in retirement.

## What If You Built Instead of Bought?

A small modular home creates another option.

Rather than selling the family property and buying a smaller apartment, an owner with suitable land could potentially build a compact home on the property they already own.

A 30 m² home can provide the essentials for two people without the space and maintenance of a large family house.

Depending on local planning rules, the existing property could potentially have another use.

The important point is that the land and location are retained.

## A Home Base for a Different Life

For some empty nesters, the ideal retirement may involve more travel.

Perhaps a few months in Spain.

A summer at the family cottage.

Long trips to visit children and grandchildren.

The rest of the year, a small home can provide a comfortable base to return to.

A compact, easy-to-maintain property can therefore be less about **downsizing your life** and more about making your home fit a life with fewer fixed responsibilities.

## Downsizing Does Not Have to Mean Moving Away

There is no universal answer to what an empty nester should do.

Some will want to sell the family home and move into an apartment.

Others will want to stay exactly where they are.

And for some, a smaller independent home on land they already own could offer a middle ground.

**Keep the location. Keep the connection to the family property. But live in a home that fits the next stage of life.**

That may be one of the more interesting ways to think about downsizing.
